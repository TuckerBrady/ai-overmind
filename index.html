Instead of one assistant that forgets everything between conversations, you get a small team. Each one has a job, remembers it, and picks up where they left off.

You say what you need in ordinary words. The right teammate gets the assignment, does the work, and tells you when it is finished.

How to start What is it, exactly?

01The problem

A helper who forgets everything is only half a helper.

If you have used an AI chat assistant, you already know it can write, summarize, plan, and answer almost anything. You have probably also run into the same three walls everyone does.

Wall 1

You explain yourself again every time

Each new conversation starts with a blank slate. Who your team is, how your projects work, what you decided last week: all of it has to be typed out again before anything useful happens.

Wall 2

One helper cannot be an expert in everything

Ask it to juggle your calendar, your budget, your projects, and your reports all at once and it does all of them at a surface level. Depth in one area comes at the cost of the others.

Wall 3

You are the only one keeping track

Nothing gets handed off unless you hand it off. Nothing gets checked unless you check it. Every reminder, every follow up, every "where did that land?" comes back to you.

Think of it as having one very smart temp who shows up with amnesia every morning. Capable, but you manage all of it.

02What it is

A way to give yourself a staff instead of a single helper.

ai-overmind is a free add on for Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic. Once you add it, Claude can do something it could not do before: build you a team and then manage it for you.

It starts by asking about your work. Then it suggests a handful of teammates who would actually help, gives each one a name and a job, and sets everything up so they are ready to go. One might handle your schedule and follow ups. Another might track your projects and write the status updates. Another might dig through numbers and explain what they mean.

The important part is that each teammate keeps their own memory of their own job. The one who tracks your projects knows your projects deeply, because that is all it thinks about. It never gets crowded out by everything else you asked for that week.

One of them acts as the manager. You tell the manager what you need, in plain words, and it decides who should handle it and writes up the assignment for them. You do not have to know who does what.

You describe the problem. It decides who handles it. You approve. That is the whole rhythm.

03Who it helps

If you juggle more than one kind of work, it helps.
If you lead people

The status chasing goes away. Ask for an update on where everything stands and get a real answer, without pinging six people or opening four tools. Reports and summaries get drafted before you ask twice.

If you build things

The work around the work stops eating your day. Keeping tickets honest, writing the documentation you have been putting off, chasing down why a test started failing, prepping the release checklist nobody wants to own. Give each of those to a teammate whose whole job is that one thing, and stay in the part you are actually good at. There is a section below on what this looks like in a codebase.

If you run projects

Nothing quietly falls off. Assignments get written down, progress is tracked in one place, and deadlines get watched for you instead of by you.

If you work for yourself

You get the staff you cannot hire. Someone to keep the books straight, someone to chase the paperwork, someone to write the thing you have been avoiding.

If you just have a lot going on

You stop being the only one holding it all. Trips, appointments, family logistics, the forms nobody wants to fill out. Hand a piece of it to someone whose only job is that piece.

04If you write code

A second engineer who reads everything and never gets tired.

Your team can include teammates who work in your codebase. Not to replace you, and not to merge anything on their own. To do the parts of engineering that are genuinely important and genuinely tedious, which is the exact combination that gets skipped when a sprint gets tight.

Review

Reads every change

A reviewer whose only job is reading diffs, and who is as careful on the last one of the day as the first. It catches the boring failures: the unhandled error path, the query inside a loop, the edge case nobody wrote down.

Tests

Writes the tests you were going to skip

Describe the behavior you want covered and get the cases back, including the ugly ones you would have left for later. Later being the release where they matter.

Docs

Keeps documentation from rotting

The README and the runbook get updated when the code changes, instead of six months later when somebody gets paged at two in the morning and finds out they are wrong.

Triage

Works out why it broke

Reads the failure, traces it back, and hands you a written explanation with the cause. You start from a diagnosis instead of a log file.

You do not stop being the engineer. You spend more of the day reviewing and deciding, and less of it typing boilerplate and reading logs.

If you are skeptical, that is a reasonable place to start. Most developers have been handed an AI tool that produced something confidently wrong and cost them an afternoon. Two things are different here. Every teammate works from a written assignment with the context and the constraints spelled out, so it is not guessing at what you meant. And nothing it produces goes anywhere until you have read it and said so. You remain the reviewer of record on your own code, which is exactly where you should be.

05How it works, day to day

Four steps. After the first one, your part is a single word.

One trip around the loop. Your part is steps 1 and 3.

1

Introduce yourself, once

You say your name and it asks a few questions about your work. Then it proposes your team and sets all of them up for you. This happens one time, and takes about ten minutes.

You typeSarah is online
2

Ask for what you need, in normal words

No special commands and no need to know who handles it. The manager works out whose job it is and writes them a complete assignment, including the background they need and what finished looks like.

You typeI need last quarter's numbers written up for the board
OrFind out why the nightly build broke and write up what happened
3

Say go

You open that teammate's window and type one word. They already know the assignment, so there is nothing to re-explain and nothing to paste in.

You type/go
4

The work comes back to you

They do the job and save the result. Something keeps an eye on it in the background and lets your manager know the moment it is done, or if it gets stuck. You find out because you were told, not because you remembered to check.

08What it is not

The honest limits, so nothing surprises you later.
  • It does not run off on its own. Nobody starts working until you say go. It will not send messages, spend money, or act on your accounts while you are not looking.
  • It is not a website you sign up for. There is no account, no subscription, and no company collecting your information. Your team lives on your own computer as ordinary files you can open and read.
  • It is not completely hands off to set up. You spend about ten minutes at the start opening a window for each teammate and pasting in what it gives you. It can check its own work afterward and tell you if you missed a step.
  • It does not replace your judgment. A teammate will sometimes tell you that what you asked for was the wrong thing to ask for. That is on purpose, and it means you still need to read what comes back.
  • You do need Claude. This adds to Claude, it does not replace it. If you do not use Claude yet, that is the first step.

09How to start

Three steps, and the last one is a sentence.
  1. Open Claude on your computer. Either the Claude desktop app or Claude Code. If you are not sure which you have, the desktop app is the one most people use.
  2. Add ai-overmind from the plugin menu. In the desktop app: Customize, then Plugins, then Add Marketplace, then paste in TuckerBrady/ai-overmind and install it.
  3. Start a new conversation and introduce yourself. Type [Your name] is online. It takes over from there and walks you through building your team.

Free to use. Nothing to sign up for. If you get stuck, the field manual walks through each step in more detail.
